# What Thoughtstead is

A plain explanation of the product. No build instructions, no constraints —
just what the thing is and what it does.

---

## In one line

**Thoughtstead is a life operating system: one place that holds everything you
are responsible for, works out how it connects, and does the follow-through.**

It is a hosted subscription. We run one instance, subscribers sign in to it.
Nothing is installed, nothing is deployed, and there is no API key to obtain —
AI usage is part of the price.

## Who it is for

**Product people building AI features and AI systems.**

The person it is written for is being asked to ship something with AI in it and
has no honest account of what it costs when it is wrong. They have research they
have read, positioning they keep re-deriving from scratch, a meeting where scope
quietly changed, a decision nobody wrote down, concepts in three files that
disagree, and a deck from March. All of it in different tools, none of which talk
to each other.

## The problem it solves

Every piece of the thing you are building lives somewhere else, so the only
place it is ever whole is in your head.

Figma will hold your drawing. Linear will hold the ticket. Notion will hold the
doc. None of them will tell you the feature should not ship — and none of them
does anything at all while you are not looking.

## How it works

### 1. You capture. It connects.

Capture is **raw-first and never lost**: you say or type one sentence and it
lands immediately. Enrichment reads it afterwards and links the people,
projects, concepts and decisions it touches. You wire nothing and you maintain
no templates.

The result is a graph you did not build. A customer call → the decision it
produced → the concept that changed → the project carrying the work → the deck
you showed. Ask why you decided something in March and you get the call it came
out of and the thinking behind it.

### 2. It gives you a method, not a blank canvas

Seven surfaces, in the order the work actually runs:

| Surface | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Research Notes** | What you have read, filed by sector, searchable by meaning |
| **Positioning** | A brief that stops being re-derived, kept current by a working session rather than a document |
| **Concepts** | One record seen two ways — a **storyboard** of what a person experiences, and a **digital twin** of the typed components behind it, linked moment to component |
| **Value Matrix** | Whether the feature should exist at all, in true and false positives and negatives, against a human baseline, with a break-even and a sensitivity curve |
| **AI Security** | What the system may touch, what it must never do unattended, what happens when the model is confidently wrong |
| **Gallery** | The deck, the one-pager, the thing you actually show someone |
| **Agentic Systems** | Whether the one you already shipped is still working |

Two of these are worth naming properly:

**The Value Matrix** is a real confusion matrix, not a metaphor. Prediction down
the rows, actual outcome across the columns. It asks how many cases were
evaluated, how many were genuinely positive, how many the system flagged, and
how many of those flags were right — then derives the four cells. It **refuses
impossible input rather than clamping it**: claim you caught 2,000 problems on
50 alerts and it tells you why that evaluation cannot exist and which number to
move. Then it puts a figure on the assumption the decision actually rests on:
*"this stops being worth shipping the moment one false alarm costs more than
$1,464."* Somebody in the room can confirm or refute that. Nobody can check a
net of $412,000.

**Concepts** are typed. A canvas offering "box" and "arrow" produces a picture
that looks like a system and answers no question about one — you cannot ask
whether a prediction has the data it needs if the prediction is a rectangle. Of
the twenty-odd component types, exactly three carry a twelve-question AI
contract: the model, the prediction, and an agent. Those are the three making a
call you will have to defend. Retrieval does not, because a lookup makes no
judgement.

Concepts are multiplayer — presence, live cursors, and comments pinned to the
point on the canvas they are about. A share link puts someone outside on the
real surfaces, read-only, and signing in through it grants them no membership. A
second kind of link invites someone in properly.

### 3. It acts, and shows the receipts

Agents brief you on the morning, chase what is overdue, and research a company
and come back with findings and the evidence attached.

**Anything outbound parks for your approval** — with its proposal attached, in a
queue. That is enforced in the database, not requested in a prompt. Every step
an agent takes leaves a record you can read back.

## What makes it a system rather than a tool

**Contexts.** Each thing you run is its own world — the product you are
building, the company, the household — isolated at the database level. Search in
one never returns the other, and an agent working in one cannot see into the
other. Same brain underneath, a different instrument on top: a context knows
what kind of thing it is and the surfaces reshape to it.

That is what earns the word "life". It does not stop at the office door, and
shipping does not pause because a warranty ran out. Alongside the studio, the
same system holds meetings and their decisions, documents filed and categorised
on arrival, projects, contacts, receivables and contracts — and, in a context of
its own, health appointments, home and auto upkeep, and warranties.

## Where you use it

**The web app is the product.** Every feature lands there first, because it is
the only surface everyone can reach — Windows, Android, Linux, a borrowed
laptop.

**Native apps do what a browser genuinely cannot.** The Mac app can sit in your
meeting and listen; it records, transcribes, and files the result against the
project it was about. The phone catches what happens away from the desk. They
are not second copies of the web app.

Full export, one click, any time — the export exists so that leaving is cheap,

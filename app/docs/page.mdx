# Start here

Thoughtstead is a life operating system. It holds what you are building — the
research, the positioning, the concepts, and the decision about whether a
feature should exist at all — alongside the company and the rest of your life,
without letting any of them see into the others.

This page is the map. Everything else in these docs hangs off it.

## Getting started

Thoughtstead is a hosted service. There is nothing to install on a server,
nothing to deploy, and no API key to obtain — AI usage is part of the
subscription. Sign in and your contexts, defaults, and document categories are
created for you on first sign-in.

From signing in to your first capture is under a minute.

## Contexts

Everything in Thoughtstead lives in a **context**. Business is one. Personal is
another. You can add more — a second company, a side project, a household.

A context is a wall, not a folder. Search in one never returns results from
another, and an agent working in one cannot see into another. That separation
is enforced in the database rather than by you remembering which app you were
supposed to have open, which is what makes it safe to keep your accounts
receivable and your dentist appointments in the same product.

Switch contexts from the context switcher; manage them in
**Settings → Contexts**.

## What lives in each

A product context gets the studio: research notes, a positioning brief,
concepts as storyboard and digital twin, the Value Matrix, AI Security, the
gallery of what you show people, and a dashboard for the systems already
shipped. A business context gets receivables, clients, contracts, projects,
meetings, decisions, documents, and people. Personal gets health appointments
and checkups, home and auto upkeep, warranties, and lifestyle plans. All of
them get the same capture, the same semantic search, and the same agents.

## Where you use it

- **The web app** is the product. Every feature lands here first, and it runs
  on any machine you can open a browser on.
- **The Mac app** does what a browser genuinely cannot: record and transcribe
  the meeting you are sitting in, and stay a keystroke away for a thought you
  need to get down.
- **Your phone** catches whatever happens away from the desk.
